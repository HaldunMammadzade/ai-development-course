# Lindel logo and visual identity exploration

Status: exploration record, preserved below. The formal identity, tokens, and SVG geometry are in `docs/brand/design-system.md` and `docs/brand/assets/`. No logo is adopted in the application. The public product name remains Müəllim.

Working name: Lindel. Finalist, not legally cleared. The public product name remains Müəllim until a later decision.

Date: 8 October 2026.

This is a visual specification for an AI-native developer education company that should still look credible if the company later outgrows courses. The product teaches frontend, full-stack, backend, AI engineering, system design, interviews, and career development. The feeling to reach is the polish of Linear, Vercel, Cursor, Notion, Arc, and Brilliant. Their marks, colors, and layouts are not a source to copy.

## Brand personality

Primary traits: intelligent, calm, precise, premium, modern, technical, human, confident, focused, aspirational.

The identity should feel like a tool a serious developer keeps open, and like a teacher who does not raise their voice. Progress is the emotion. Decoration is not.

Out of bounds: childish illustration, gamer neon, hacker terminals, cyberpunk, generic AI gradients and orbs, heavy 3D, cartoon mascots, consulting-firm crests, and the soft illustration style of a generic education startup.

## How to read the scores

Each direction is scored 1–10. The weighted score uses these weights, which sum to 100:

| Criterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Memorability | 10 |
| Simplicity | 10 |
| 16px readability | 12 |
| App icon potential | 8 |
| Favicon potential | 8 |
| Wordmark compatibility | 7 |
| Premium feel | 10 |
| Developer relevance | 5 |
| Education relevance | 4 |
| AI relevance | 3 |
| Future scalability | 8 |
| Motion potential | 5 |
| Distinctiveness | 6 |
| Global suitability | 4 |

Education and AI are weighted low on purpose. A mark that only means “school” or “model” will not survive a larger company. Small-size readability and premium feel are weighted high because the mark has to live in a browser tab, a dock, and a dashboard.

Weighted score = sum of (score × weight) / 100.

## Directions

### A. The Step

1. Concept. Two horizontal bars, left-aligned. The upper bar is shorter. The lower bar is longer. Nothing else.
2. Symbol meaning. The short bar is the current reach. The long bar is the next standard. Learning, then progress, then mastery, without drawing a path.
3. Geometry. One stroke weight. One gap equal to that stroke. Shared left edge. No taper, no radius larger than a quarter of the stroke, no second color inside the mark.
4. Wordmark treatment. Icon at the left, optically centered on the two bars. Wordmark in lowercase. The long bar and the wordmark baseline do not have to align; the whole stack centers on the wordmark’s x-height.
5. Icon behavior. The symbol is the company. It never grows a third bar, an arrow, or a dot.
6. Small-size behavior. At 16px the length difference must stay at least 4px. Equal bars become a menu or an equals sign and are a failed drawing.
7. Dark-background behavior. Bars in off-white. No glow, no gradient.
8. Light-background behavior. Bars in near-black. Same geometry. No drop shadow.
9. App icon potential. Strong. The two lengths read inside a square with generous padding.
10. Favicon potential. Strong. Two rectangles survive 16px if the stroke is 2px and the gap is 2px.
11. Animation potential. The short bar draws, then the long bar draws, from the shared left edge. A loading state shortens and restores only the short bar.
12. Why it fits Lindel. The name does not depict a subject. The mark depicts becoming better at the work. It still means something if the catalog grows past coding.

Weighted score: **8.32**. Primary direction.

### B. Foundation

1. Concept. One wide, low block. A second, shorter block sits on it, sharing the left edge, like a course of stone seen from the side.
2. Symbol meaning. Fundamentals first. A developer is only as steady as the base.
3. Geometry. The lower block is about twice the height of a Step stroke, so it reads as mass, not as a line. The upper block is the Step’s short bar, seated with no gap or with a hairline joint.
4. Wordmark treatment. The mass wants to sit on the baseline beside the word, which makes the lockup bottom-heavy.
5. Icon behavior. Usable alone. At a glance it is a plinth, which is a quiet architectural reference and not a building.
6. Small-size behavior. The joint disappears at 16px and the mark becomes one lumpy rectangle, or it separates and becomes the Step with a fatter base.
7. Dark-background behavior. Solid off-white shapes. A hairline joint is invisible on dark and should not be relied on.
8. Light-background behavior. Solid near-black. Same problem: the joint only works above 32px.
9. App icon potential. Acceptable, slightly heavy. It can look like a battery or a handle.
10. Favicon potential. Weak. Mass without a gap fills in.
11. Animation potential. Low. A block has little honest motion except a slow settle, which feels decorative.
12. Why it fits Lindel. The company is about fundamentals. The mark says “base” more loudly than “progress,” and the name Stylobate was already rejected for that metaphor. The idea can inform the Step’s lower bar. It should not be the logo.

Weighted score: **6.66**.

### C. Path

1. Concept. A single open polyline of two segments, rising from left to right. No arrowhead. Ends are cut square.
2. Symbol meaning. A learning journey that changes level. Career as a change in elevation, not a destination pin.
3. Geometry. Constant stroke. One corner only. The rise is about half the run, so it is not a checkmark and not a chart spike.
4. Wordmark treatment. The rising end fights a horizontal wordmark. The lockup looks like a trend line next to a name.
5. Icon behavior. The line needs padding or the corner clips. Alone, it is easy to read as analytics.
6. Small-size behavior. A 1px or 2px stroke at 16px breaks or looks like a check. Fails the tab test.
7. Dark-background behavior. Off-white stroke. Thin strokes halate on dark displays and need to be thicker than the design wants.
8. Light-background behavior. Near-black stroke. Slightly safer, still cramped at 16px.
9. App icon potential. Weak. A diagonal in a square is a generic “growth” app.
10. Favicon potential. Poor.
11. Animation potential. A draw-on along the path is clear and still looks like every product-tour line.
12. Why it fits Lindel. The curriculum is a progression. The drawing is too close to a sparkline, a check, and a swoosh to be ownable.

Weighted score: **5.81**.

### D. Layers

1. Concept. Three horizontal bars of equal length, stacked, each shifted right by one stroke. Same left-edge family as the Step, but the meaning is depth rather than length.
2. Symbol meaning. Concept, practice, mastery. Three passes over the same material.
3. Geometry. Equal bars, equal gaps, equal offset. Three is the maximum. Two collapses into the Step. Four becomes a list.
4. Wordmark treatment. A tall stack next to a short word. The icon becomes heavier than “lindel.”
5. Icon behavior. Works as a stack. Too easy to see a database, a hamburger, or a layer panel.
6. Small-size behavior. Three 2px bars plus two gaps already consume 10px before padding. At 16px the offsets vanish and it is a menu icon.
7. Dark-background behavior. Off-white. If the rear bars are dimmed, it looks disabled.
8. Light-background behavior. Near-black. Same menu problem.
9. App icon potential. Average. Familiar in a way that does not help.
10. Favicon potential. Weak once the third bar is honest.
11. Animation potential. A layer can reveal from back to front. That motion is good and the resting mark is not.
12. Why it fits Lindel. Practice over the same idea is the product. The resting symbol is a common SaaS stack. Rejected as a logo, kept as a motion reference only.

Weighted score: **6.40**.

### E. Open L

1. Concept. An abstract L. The stem is short. The foot is long. They do not meet: a gap the size of the stroke sits at the corner, so it is not a closed corporate letter.
2. Symbol meaning. The letter in the name, drawn with the same rule as the Step. The gap is the unfinished part of the skill.
3. Geometry. Stem height equals the foot’s extra length past the stem. Stroke matches the Step. Corner is open on the inside. No serif, no spur, no rounded join.
4. Wordmark treatment. Risky. The icon already says L, and the word begins with L. The lockup stutters.
5. Icon behavior. Excellent alone. This is the best of the set as a pure app icon.
6. Small-size behavior. At 16px the gap must stay at least 2px or the mark closes into a normal L. Buildable, less forgiving than two bars.
7. Dark-background behavior. Off-white. The gap is the whole idea and must stay visible.
8. Light-background behavior. Near-black. Same rule.
9. App icon potential. Strong.
10. Favicon potential. Good if the gap survives.
11. Animation potential. The foot can draw after the stem, which is the Step’s motion turned on its side.
12. Why it fits Lindel. It ties the mark to the name without a mascot. It is less scalable than a non-letter symbol if the company ever wants a mark that is not an initial. Third direction, not the primary, because the wordmark lockup doubles the L.

Weighted score: **7.17**. Third direction.

### F. The Pair

1. Concept. Two separate bars on one baseline, with a gap between them. The left bar is short and slightly taller. The right bar is long and lower. They do not touch. No line connects them.
2. Symbol meaning. Student and mentor. The relationship is alignment and distance, not a speech bubble or a figure.
3. Geometry. Shared baseline. Gap equals the short bar’s width. Height difference is one stroke. No dots, no nodes, no orbit.
4. Wordmark treatment. Wide icon. It pushes the wordmark too far right in a navbar.
5. Icon behavior. Readable as a relationship only when someone is told. Otherwise it is two leftovers.
6. Small-size behavior. The height difference collapses at 16px. The gap looks like a broken wordmark or a pause control.
7. Dark-background behavior. Off-white. Fine when large.
8. Light-background behavior. Near-black. Fine when large.
9. App icon potential. Fair. The empty middle wastes the square.
10. Favicon potential. Poor. Reads as a glitch.
11. Animation potential. One bar can ease toward the other and stop short. Easy to overdo. If they meet, the meaning dies.
12. Why it fits Lindel. Mentorship is the product promise. The drawing is less immediate than the Step and fails the tab. Rejected as a logo. The “do not touch” rule is kept: the Step’s bars also never join into an E or an equals sign.

Weighted score: **6.60**.

### G. The Cut

1. Concept. A square field with one vertical division, offset from center. The left field is narrower. The right field is wider. No pictogram of school or code. The meaning is the proportion.
2. Symbol meaning. None at first look. The brand story supplies it: you move from the narrower measure to the wider one. Useful if Lindel later sells more than education.
3. Geometry. Square. One cut. Left width : right width = 5 : 8. The cut is a gap of one stroke, not a line, so the mark is two fields, not a window.
4. Wordmark treatment. Calm beside lowercase “lindel.” The square can also sit above the word in a stacked lockup.
5. Icon behavior. Strong alone. It does not explain the product, which is the point.
6. Small-size behavior. At 16px the gap must remain 2px and the 5:8 split must stay obvious. Achievable. Less obvious than two bars of different length.
7. Dark-background behavior. Off-white fields on near-black, or the fields knocked out of a solid tile.
8. Light-background behavior. Near-black fields on off-white.
9. App icon potential. Strong. A square mark inside a square icon needs extra padding so it does not look like a full-bleed block.
10. Favicon potential. Good. Simpler than a letter, stricter than a picture.
11. Animation potential. The cut can travel from 5 toward 8 and return. That motion is abstract. Use it once, not as a idle loop.
12. Why it fits Lindel. The company may outgrow “coding school.” A proportion can stay. It is the backup because, without the story, it is quieter than the Step and easier to confuse with a layout glyph.

Weighted score: **7.55**. Backup direction.

## Wordmark

Case.

| Case | Reading | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lindel` | Quiet, current, technical | Use this |
| `LINDEL` | Institutional, louder, better at a distance on signage | Reserve for legal lines and tiny all-caps labels, not the logo |
| `Lindel` | Friendly, closer to an education startup | Do not use in the logo |

Lowercase wins because the brand has to feel precise rather than ceremonial, and title case is the default of companies that still look like courses.

Skeleton. Draw the wordmark from a modern grotesk: even stroke, large counters, low contrast, unbracketed terminals. That genre keeps `l`, `i`, `n`, `d`, `e`, and `l` on one rhythm. A humanist sans (the current product face, Source Sans 3) is warmer and better for long reading than for a six-letter mark. A geometric sans with circular bowls makes `d` and `e` cute. Do not pick a display serif. Do not invent a random free font for the logo and a second one for the app.

Letter rules.

- `L` / `l`. A plain stem. No spur. The logo’s first stem and last stem match.
- `i`. Square dot, about 0.85 of the stem width, separated from the stem by a gap close to the dot’s size. A round dot is softer than the bars.
- `n`. Same shoulder height as `d`. Straight right stem.
- `d`. Bowl height equals `e`. Ascender aligns with `l` and `i`.
- `e`. Horizontal bar optically centered, not mathematically centered. Large counter.
- Final `l`. Same stem as the first. The pair `ll` is the risk: two stems clot. Open the space between them by about one fifth of a stem relative to the other letter-pairs. Do not slant one of them. Do not join them.

Tracking of the whole word is slightly open, not tight. Tight grotesk tracking makes `ll` look like a wall.

Until a custom drawing exists, the interim wordmark face is Inter Tight at medium weight, with the `ll` pair opened by hand. Inter is the UI face below. One family, two jobs. A licensed grotesk such as Neue Haas Grotesk can replace Inter Tight later for the wordmark only. That replacement is a refinement, not a different logo.

## Typography

Three roles. Not five families.

| Role | Face | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display / wordmark | Inter Tight, medium, custom `ll` spacing. Later optional: a licensed even grotesk for the wordmark only | Even stems, premium at logo size, same skeleton as the UI |
| UI | Inter | Dashboard reading, Latin extended for Azerbaijani, one weight system, no display tricks in product chrome |
| Code | IBM Plex Mono | Readable at 13–14px, credible in a lesson, less costume-like than a hacker bitmap face |

Source Sans 3 and Source Serif 4 are what the product uses today. They stay until an implementation step. This document does not change them. Source Serif is a good reading face and a poor logo face. It is not part of the Lindel mark.

Weights in UI: regular for body, medium for titles, semibold only for the rare emphasis. No black weights. No italic as a brand voice.

## Motion

Motion shows progress. It does not decorate.

| Use | Duration | Curve | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UI state change | 160–200ms | ease-out | Opacity and color only |
| Panel, drawer, step change | 220–280ms | ease-out | Transform at most 8px, plus opacity |
| Logo entrance | 220ms then 220ms | ease-out | Short bar draws from the left edge, then the long bar. 40ms overlap |
| Loading | 700ms loop | linear | Short bar moves between 55% and 100% of its length. Long bar stays still |
| Reduced motion | 0ms | none | Final geometry immediately |

No bounce, no spring overshoot, no constant background motion, no shimmer, no particle, no rotating mark. A lesson completing may lengthen the short bar once. It does not fire a burst.

The Cut’s only motion is the gap easing a few units and returning, under 300ms, and only when the mark enters. The Open L’s only motion is the foot drawing outward.

## Brand stories

### The Step

Sentence. Lindel is the distance between the work you can do and the work you are about to do.

Paragraph. A course can be watched and left unchanged. Lindel stays on the attempt. The short bar is where you are. The long bar is the standard you have not met yet. When the work gets better, the short bar lengthens. The mark does not celebrate a finish line. It keeps a next length in view.

Visual metaphor. Two measures, one origin.

Emotional meaning. Calm ambition. You are not behind. You are short of a length you can see.

Product connection. Lessons, projects, reviews, interviews, and career steps all use the same figure: try, miss, understand, lengthen.

### The Cut

Sentence. Lindel is a wider measure than the one you arrived with.

Paragraph. The mark is only a proportion. Nothing in it is a classroom or a compiler. The company can teach coding now and still be a technology company later, because the symbol never drew the subject. People learn what it means by using the product: the narrower field is the current skill, the wider field is the one you can grow into.

Visual metaphor. One square, divided off center.

Emotional meaning. Room. The work has a larger side.

Product connection. Useful on an app icon and a future corporate mark. Weaker inside a lesson, where the Step explains progress faster.

### Open L

Sentence. The name already contains the step.

Paragraph. Lindel begins and ends with the same stem. The icon opens that letter and refuses to close the corner. The gap is deliberate: a skill that looks finished from a distance still has a joint to make. It is the most name-bound of the three and the least flexible if the symbol ever has to leave the word.

Visual metaphor. An L that does not quite connect.

Emotional meaning. Precision, and a little unfinished business.

Product connection. Best as the app icon if the Step ever feels too quiet in a store listing. Weaker beside the full wordmark.

The line under all three: you do not just consume courses. You become a better developer.

## Primary specification

Direction: The Step. This is a construction spec, not an asset.

Geometry. Left-aligned horizontal bars. Upper bar shorter. Lower bar longer. Shared left edge. Constant stroke. Gap equals stroke. Corners square, or with a radius no greater than one quarter of the stroke. At 16px, radius is 0.

Proportions on a 16-unit grid.

- Stroke: 2
- Gap: 2
- Left inset: 2
- Short bar: x 2, y 5, width 7, height 2
- Long bar: x 2, y 9, width 12, height 2
- Right inset against the long bar: 2

The short bar is 7/12 of the long bar. That ratio is the master. Do not center the short bar. A centered short bar becomes a staircase, a server, or a pause glyph.

Construction rules.

- Two shapes only.
- Same color. No accent inside the logo.
- No outline version with a gap fill.
- No third bar, tick, dot, or arrow.
- Do not rotate it into a play button or an L in the primary system. The Open L is a separate direction.
- Optical correction: none at this size. If a large sign makes the lower bar look heavier, shorten it by less than half a stroke. Do not change the 7/12 ratio in product UI.

Clear space. One stroke (2 units on this grid) on every side, including between icon and wordmark. No type, rule, or badge inside that margin.

Minimum size. Icon 16px. Wordmark cap height 12px in UI, 16px preferred. Below 16px, do not draw the icon.

Icon and wordmark. Horizontal lockup. Order: icon, clear space, `lindel`. Vertical center of the two-bar stack aligns to the vertical center of the lowercase word. Stacked lockup, icon above word, is allowed for square avatars and the app icon only.

Monochrome. One color. Off-white `#F3F4F6` on dark. Near-black `#12141A` on light. No blue logo.

Dark version. Bars `#F3F4F6` on `#0C0D10`. App tile uses the same background, not a lighter chip.

Light version. Bars `#12141A` on `#F6F7F8` or white. Do not put a dark tile behind the mark on a light page.

Favicon. 16px uses the grid above, radius 0. 32px doubles every number. No wordmark. No container circle.

App icon. Square artwork. Platform masks may squircle it. Padding from the tile edge to the long bar is 22% of the tile. Background is `#0C0D10` in the dark listing and `#F6F7F8` in the light listing. No gradient, no gloss, no wordmark inside the icon.

Animation. On first appearance: short bar draws from x=2 to its full width in 220ms, ease-out; long bar starts 180ms after the short bar and draws in 220ms. Loading: long bar static; short bar width moves from 55% to 100% and back over 700ms, linear. A completed step in the product may play the entrance once. Reduced motion shows the finished mark.

## Rejected concepts

Rejected as logos, and why.

- Foundation. The joint dies at 16px, and the mass looks like a battery. The “base” metaphor was already retired with the name Stylobate.
- Path. Reads as a chart, a check, or a swoosh. Fails the favicon.
- Layers. Three equal bars become a menu or a database. The reveal motion is worth borrowing. The symbol is not.
- The Pair. Mentorship is real, and the icon looks broken at 16px. Connecting the bars with a line would make a generic network mark, which is also rejected.
- Open L as the primary lockup. Strong as an app icon, weak next to the word because the L repeats. Kept as the third direction, not the main logo.
- The Cut as the primary. More scalable, less immediate. Kept as the backup.
- Graduation cap, mortarboard, book, pencil, diploma. These say “course platform,” which is the positioning to avoid.
- Brain, robot, sparkle, star, lightbulb, rocket. Generic intelligence and generic ambition.
- Circuit, CPU, terminal, angle brackets, code braces. Hacker costume. The product is a teacher, not a prompt decoration.
- Chat bubble, person, handshake, nodes-and-lines. Mentorship drawn literally.
- Checkmark, arrow, shield, infinity. Common SaaS parts. A path that bends into a check is the same failure.
- Staircase, steps with more than two treads, chevrons. The Step is two lengths, not a stair icon.
- Bird, despite earlier nature names. Lindel is not a species.
- Gradient orbs, glass blobs, 3D extrusion, neon outlines. Generic AI and gamer signals.
- A tinted or two-color mark. Color belongs to progress in the product, not to the logo.
- Uppercase or title-case wordmarks for the primary logo. Documented above and set aside.

## What this step does not do

No change to routes, packages, UI, database, schema, or source. The application is still Müəllim. Lindel is not legally cleared. The exploration stopped before assets. The next section records what the formal specification changed.

## Formal specification

Date: 8 October 2026.

`docs/brand/design-system.md` is now the source of truth for construction, tokens, and component rules. This exploration remains the record of why The Step won and why the other directions lost. Nothing above this heading is deleted.

Geometry of The Step is unchanged: 16-unit grid, stroke 2, gap 2, left inset 2, short bar 7, long bar 12, left aligned, square corners. It was not abandoned after the distinctiveness pass.

Distinctiveness, from public pages on 8 October 2026, not a legal search:

- ElevenLabs uses three horizontal bars of different lengths as an E. A third bar would collide with that mark and with a hamburger. The Step stays at two bars.
- past.dev uses bars, including a separated third bar. Different count, different rhythm.
- DoubleSpace uses two horizontal planes cut by an upload arrow. The arrow is the thing The Step refuses.
- IconPark “align left two” is the same count and the same left edge, drawn as a thin UI stroke. The Step is not that icon: stroke equals gap, so the bars are blocks; the short bar is on top; the ratio is fixed at 7/12. Product UI that aligns text must use a separate, thinner align icon and must not reuse the logo.
- Mixpanel’s M is three vertical bars. Not this mark.
- Devlateral, Codecademy, and Pluralsight use letters, arrows, or pictorial education marks. Not this mark.
- Linear, Vercel, Cursor, Notion, Arc, and Brilliant were checked as the reference set. None of them is two left-aligned horizontal blocks.

Wordmark correction. The exploration text above said to open the space inside an `ll` pair. The word is `lindel`: l, i, n, d, e, l. The two stems are the first letter and the last letter. They are not a digraph, and the word is not Lindell. The formal spec replaces that instruction. Overall tracking stays slightly open. The only local adjustment is a small extra gap between `e` and the final `l` if that pair crowds. Stem weight of the first `l` and the last `l` matches.

Assets under `docs/brand/assets/` are geometry files for the symbol and reference lockups. The lockup wordmark is SVG text in Inter Tight, not outlined, because that font is not embedded and must not be fetched from a URL. The symbol rectangles are the normative drawing.
